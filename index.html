I work on ad auction floor-price optimization at Glance, using XGBoost and contextual bandit models across major OEMs. The goal is to increase revenue while responding to changes in how demand-side platforms bid for ad space on the Glance app.

Before Glance, I spent three years at Zepto working on demand forecasting, replenishment, routing, and assortment planning. Given a few hundred dark stores, a catalogue that turns over constantly, and customers who expect their order in ten minutes, what should each store hold, how much should we buy, and how should it all move? That work taught me a lot about building models that have to earn their place in a live operational system.

I came to this from physics, by way of telescopes. My master's thesis, with the CTLearn group at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, used recurrent networks to clean up the noisy images that gamma-ray telescopes record when cosmic particles strike the atmosphere. Before that I contributed to the open-source project through Google Summer of Code, and I studied electrical engineering and mathematics at BITS Pilani. Somewhere along the way I stopped pointing models at the sky and started pointing them at supply chains, which have more in common than you would think: both are mostly the work of pulling a signal out of a great deal of noise.

The work I care about most sits where a model meets the real world: a truck that leaves at six, a shelf that holds only so much. Most of what I know I learned by shipping things and watching them break in instructive ways.
